# Client Build #1 — Predator 212cc Governor Removal & Flywheel Upgrade

## Project Overview

A client requested a modified Predator 212cc Non-Hemi engine for use in a minibike. The requested work included removal of the factory governor. Because removing the governor changes the engine's operating conditions, I treated the job as more than simply removing one component.

The build required internal disassembly, removal of the governor and oil sensor, sealing the openings left by those components, evaluating the resulting throttle/linkage requirements, reassembling the engine, and upgrading the flywheel for higher-RPM use.

## Client Request

The client wanted the factory governor removed from the engine for a performance-oriented minibike setup.

My goal was to complete the requested modification while also addressing the mechanical consequences created by removing factory components.

## Work Performed

### 1. Pre-modification inspection

Before disassembly, I identified the internal components that would need to be removed and documented the planned work for the client.

🎥 **[Video — Pre-Modification Overview](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/olJybV_sIkk)**

### 2. Engine disassembly & governor/oil-sensor removal

I removed the necessary external and internal components to gain access to the governor and oil-sensor systems. The factory governor and oil-sensor components were then removed. I documented the open engine so the client could verify that the requested internal work had been completed.

🎥 **[Video — Governor & Oil Sensor Removed](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/dZSufybwonU)**

### 3. Throttle and linkage considerations

Removing the factory governor changes how the original throttle and linkage system operates. I explained to the client that portions of the stock carburetor/linkage arrangement would not function in the same way without additional linkage hardware or configuration changes.

🎥 **[Video — Throttle & Carburetor Linkage Explanation](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YpnuP0PxABw)**

### 4. Identifying openings created by component removal

Removing the governor and oil sensor leaves openings in the engine that cannot be left exposed. I identified these locations before reassembly and explained why they needed to be properly sealed to prevent potential oil leakage.

🎥 **[Video — Governor & Oil Sensor Openings](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/FEO9C8goLRc)**

### 5. Sealing and securing the openings

I installed plugs in the openings left by the removed components. Thread-locking compound was applied to help prevent the plugs from loosening because of engine vibration.

🎥 **[Video — Installing & Securing Engine Plugs](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/BcZdxoNGwN0)**

### 6. Internal reassembly

After the modifications were completed and the openings were sealed, the internal engine components were reinstalled and checked prior to closing the engine.

### 7. ARC billet flywheel upgrade

Because the engine could now operate above its original governed-speed range, I did not want to rely on the factory cast flywheel for the modified application.

I replaced it with an **ARC 6625 billet aluminum flywheel** designed for the Predator 212cc Non-Hemi platform and intended for high-performance, higher-RPM use.

**[ARC 6625 Billet Flywheel — Component Reference](https://www.gopowersports.com/arc-billet-flywheel-212cc-non-hemi-predator/)**

This was an important design decision because the governor modification changed the operating environment of other rotating components in the engine. Rather than viewing the governor removal as an isolated change, I considered how it affected the rest of the system.

🎥 **[Video — ARC Billet Flywheel Upgrade](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vO1HkppnFe4)**

## Testing and Result

After reassembly, the engine was installed in the client's minibike and run to verify that the completed engine operated after the modifications.

The final running test provided functional validation of the completed mechanical work and reassembly.

🎥 **[Video — Final Engine Test](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/mw3aD7MwfxM)**

## Engineering Considerations

This project demonstrates several important principles that apply beyond small engines:

- A modification to one subsystem can change the requirements of other components.
- Component removal can create secondary issues that must be addressed before reassembly.
- Higher operating speeds increase the importance of appropriate rotating-component selection.
- Mechanical modifications should account for vibration, sealing, compatibility, and system integration.
- Communicating configuration changes and limitations to the client is part of completing the job successfully.
