# Client Build #1 — Predator 212cc Governor Removal & Flywheel Upgrade

## Project Overview

For this project, a client wanted me to remove the governor from a Predator 212cc Non-Hemi engine for his minibike. I knew removing the governor was going to affect more than just one part of the engine, so I planned out the other things I would need to address while I had the engine apart.

The build included taking the engine apart, removing the governor and oil sensor, plugging the openings left behind, explaining the throttle/linkage changes to the client, putting the engine back together, and upgrading the flywheel for the higher-RPM setup.

🎥 **[Watch the Full Client Build Playlist on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vO1HkppnFe4&list=PLcigjzDv7mFA)**

## What the Client Wanted

The main thing the client asked me to do was remove the factory governor. Before starting, I went over what I planned to remove and documented the process for him as I worked on the engine.

## Build Process

### 1. Planning the work

Before taking the engine apart, I went over the components I was going to remove and what I would need to get to inside the engine.

🎥 **[Video — Pre-Modification Overview](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/olJybV_sIkk)**

### 2. Disassembly and governor/oil-sensor removal

I removed the parts needed to open the engine and get access to the governor and oil sensor. I then removed both systems and recorded the inside of the engine so the client could see that the work had been completed.

🎥 **[Video — Governor & Oil Sensor Removed](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/dZSufybwonU)**

### 3. Throttle and linkage changes

One thing I had to explain to the client was that the stock throttle and carburetor linkage would not work exactly the same after removing the governor. The original setup uses parts of the governor linkage, so the new setup would need additional linkage hardware or a different configuration.

🎥 **[Video — Throttle & Carburetor Linkage Explanation](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YpnuP0PxABw)**

### 4. Plugging the openings

Removing the governor and oil sensor leaves openings in the engine. I did not want to put everything back together with those openings exposed because they could become oil-leak points.

I first showed the client where the openings were and why they needed to be plugged.

🎥 **[Video — Governor & Oil Sensor Openings](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/FEO9C8goLRc)**

### 5. Securing the plugs

I installed plugs in the openings and used Loctite thread locker to help keep them from backing out from engine vibration.

🎥 **[Video — Installing & Securing Engine Plugs](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/BcZdxoNGwN0)**

### 6. Reassembly

Once the governor and oil sensor were removed and the openings were sealed, I put the internal engine components back together and checked the setup before closing the engine.

### 7. ARC billet flywheel upgrade

This was one of the most important decisions I made on the build. With the governor removed, the engine could operate above its original governed RPM range. Because of that, I did not want to keep the stock cast flywheel in a setup intended to run at higher RPM.

I replaced it with an **ARC 6625 billet aluminum flywheel** made for the Predator 212cc Non-Hemi platform and intended for performance applications.

**[ARC 6625 Billet Flywheel — Component Reference](https://www.gopowersports.com/arc-billet-flywheel-212cc-non-hemi-predator/)**

To me, this was a good example of how changing one part of a system can affect other parts. Removing the governor did what the client wanted, but it also meant I needed to think about the components that would now be operating under different conditions.

🎥 **[Video — ARC Billet Flywheel Upgrade](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vO1HkppnFe4)**

## Final Test

After I finished the engine and put everything back together, it was installed in the client's minibike and run to make sure the engine operated after the work was completed.

🎥 **[Video — Final Engine Test](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/mw3aD7MwfxM)**

## What I Learned / Applied

This build involved more than just taking a governor out. I had to think about what else changed because of the modification, including sealing the engine, the throttle linkage, vibration, component compatibility, and the flywheel.

It also gave me experience communicating with a client throughout a build. I sent videos during the process to show what I had completed and explain things the client would need to know about the new setup.

## Skills Used

- Small-engine disassembly and reassembly
- Internal engine modification
- Mechanical troubleshooting
- Component selection and compatibility
- Identifying and sealing possible oil-leak points
- Using thread-locking compound
- Throttle/linkage evaluation
- Client communication
- Final operational testing
